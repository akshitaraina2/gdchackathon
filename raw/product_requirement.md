Problem. In rural and low-resource areas, people with a health concern often don't know whether it's something to manage at home, something needing a clinic visit, or a genuine emergency. That uncertainty leads to two bad outcomes: dangerous delays when care is urgent, and overwhelmed clinics when it isn't.
Users. People in rural or underserved communities (and the community health workers who help them) who have basic phone/web access but limited immediate access to a doctor.
Core flow (the one thing we build). User describes their symptoms in plain language → app returns a clear risk level (Self-care / See a clinic soon / Seek urgent care now) → plus 2–3 concrete next steps for that level.
Features (ruthlessly scoped for the demo):

A simple symptom input screen (free text, plain language).
A triage result screen showing the risk level with clear visual coding (green / amber / red) and short, actionable guidance.
A persistent "This is guidance, not a diagnosis — seek professional care when in doubt" line.

Out of scope for v1 (say this to judges — it shows deliberate choices): no login, no voice input, no real patient data, no clinic database. Sample/AI-generated triage logic only.
Success for the demo. A judge can type a symptom, see a sensible risk level with next steps in under 10 seconds, and immediately understand who it helps and why.
Impact (SDG 3 angle). Reduces dangerous delays and unnecessary clinic load by helping people make the right first decision — closing a gap that's widest exactly where doctors are scarcest.
